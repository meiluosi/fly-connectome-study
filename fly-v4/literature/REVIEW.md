# Literature audit trims overreaching mushroom-body claims

## 中文摘要

本报告综合五份研究笔记，检验一项"真实果蝇蘑菇体连接组（PN->KC->MBON）是否构成有用归纳偏置"的方法学/负结果研究所依赖的文献主张。主要结论：(1) "KC 接线接近随机"只能表述为"在非均匀糖球概率与 KC 类型依赖偏置下近似随机，另有小幅、类别特异的结构"（Zheng 2022 的 z=13.8 核心群落过度汇聚；但局部随机零模型几乎可解释之）；(2) "糖球层面输入总量跨个体守恒"没有任何已读文献支持，且与 Dorkenwald/Schlegel/Zheng 的变异性表述相悖；(3) "PAM=奖励、PPL1=惩罚"是一阶近似，原作者自己用"generally/preferentially"限定，且 γ3、β2/β'2(MB-M3)、γ5、α'3 等存在已发表的例外；(4) "多巴胺编码预测误差"强于证据：两项直接阻断实验均未发现 blocking，Zhao 2021 仅有 28 个数据点；(5) Lin 2014 只显示相似气味对受损，相异对的检验为不显著（OCT/MCH P=0.09），仅测 5 分钟记忆、仅两对相异气味；(6) Handler 2019 仅读到摘要，时间窗口与"前/后向配对"细节未核实，且 delta 规则本身无法产生符号反转；(7) 没有来源比较过 SLDA 与 FlyModel，原 FlyModel 论文基线很弱，该主张是项目自己的实证结果，文献既不支持也不反驳；(8) 没有发现"真实接线优于打乱接线"的 MB 研究，但检索有限，"never"应限定范围；(9) 同模型子代理评审不是独立错误过程（Kim 2025、Panickssery 2024 等）。报告还列出引用更正（如 Zheng 2022 为 Current Biology 而非 eLife；Li 2020 为 eLife 62576 而非 57443；Hige 2015 Neuron/Nature 之分；Campbell 2013 为 J Neurosci）以及无法核实的条目。所有阅读深度标签均沿用笔记原样，未作升级。

## Bottom line

Most of the project's biological premises survive only in a weakened form, and its methodological premises need explicit hedges. The notes support "approximately random PN->KC wiring with small, class-specific structure" rather than "close to random" without qualification: Caron 2013 found no structure in 200 KCs (one per fly), but Zheng 2022 shows that sample size was too small to detect the food-responsive "core community" (z=13.8), which a local-random null then largely explains ([Zheng 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9413950)). No source read supports "glomerulus-level input totals are conserved across individuals"; the sources point the other way. The PAM = reward / PPL1 = punishment partition is the canonical first-order rule, hedged by its own authors and broken at compartment level (PAM-gamma3, MB-M3 in beta2/beta'2, PAM-gamma5, PPL1-alpha'3) ([Aso 2014b](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04580); [Yamagata 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5172549)). "Dopamine signals prediction error" is stronger than the evidence: the only direct blocking tests found no blocking, and the one supportive model fit rests on 28 data points ([Zhao 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7990964/)). Lin 2014 supports a similar-pair impairment but does not prove that dissimilar discrimination is spared (OCT vs MCH, P = 0.09) ([Lin 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4000970/)). Nothing in the notes contradicts the headline negative result (no advantage for real over shuffled wiring) or the replay-free SLDA comparison, but neither is positively established by the literature, and the search behind that absence was limited. For methods, the notes warn that a 0.03 SESOI needs a noise-floor justification, that per-stage multiplicity families leave cross-stage error uncontrolled, that tuning parity and grid-edge checks must be shown, and that same-model sub-agent reviews are not independent. Every depth label below is copied from the notes; where two notes labelled the same paper differently, both labels are shown.

Read-depth key used throughout: **FULL/FT** = full text retrieved, but through a summarising fetch tool, so numbers are "as relayed"; **FT-partial/PARTIAL** = only part of the text; **ABS** = abstract or snippet only; **SEC** = secondary source; **MEM** = memory only, unverified. Several of the notes themselves warn that the fetch tool returns a model-generated summary rather than raw text.

## KC wiring is approximately random, with small structure and no verified conservation

The wiring literature has moved from "random" to "random with structure", and the two positions are less opposed than they look. Caron 2013 traced 200 KCs, one per fly (mean 7 claws, range 2-11), and tested against 1,000 shuffles that already preserved non-uniform glomerular frequencies (DA1 and DC3 were most frequent at 5.1% each); co-occurrence statistics were "consistent with shuffled data", though some were "data not shown" ([Caron 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4148081)). Zheng 2022 reconstructed 7,102 claws of 1,356 KCs (mean 5.2 claws, SD 1.6) and found a core PN community making 1,916 claws against a random-bouton null of 1,421.7 (SD 35.7), z=13.8, concentrated in alpha/beta and alpha'/beta' KCs. The qualifier matters: a spatially local random null gave 1,890.6 claws against 1,916 observed, and subsampling FAFB to Caron's size made the community undetectable ([Zheng 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9413950)). Li 2020 found three PCs exceeding its shuffle model and skew toward DP1m and DM1, but called the dimensional effect "modest", minimal for gamma KCs and not persistent at the KC->MBON level ([Li 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7909955)). The defensible wording is therefore "approximately random given non-uniform glomerular and KC-type-dependent probabilities, with small structured deviations". Whole-population models that pool KC types can miss the structure, because it sits in alpha/beta and alpha'/beta' KCs.

Heterogeneity and variability claims fare differently. In-degree heterogeneity is supported (2-11 claws in Caron 2013; SD 1.6 around 5.2 in Zheng 2022; 1-6 inputs in larval KCs per a snippet-level source). Mean claws per KC differ across datasets (5.2 FAFB, 5.6 hemibrain, "about six" in Li's introduction, 7 in Caron with a different counting method), roughly a 35% spread that counters any claim of tight conservation. Synapse-count heterogeneity per claw was **not found in any fetched text**, so it is unsupported by the reading rather than contradicted; edge thresholds (5 synapses in Li 2020, >4 in FlyWire) also shape what counts as a connection. For cross-individual conservation of glomerulus-level input totals, the notes found no supporting source. Dorkenwald 2024 relays that the companion paper reports "high variability for principal neurons of the mushroom body", while saying cell-type counts and strong connections largely agree between FlyWire and hemibrain ([Dorkenwald 2024, bioRxiv v2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10327113)). The Schlegel 2024 abstract describes "functional homeostasis in the mushroom body": absolute excitatory input changes while E/I balance is maintained, which is the opposite of conserved totals (abstract only: [record](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/330521)). FAFB and hemibrain PN-type output fractions correlate at r^2 = 0.83, substantial but incomplete agreement, and Zheng states core membership likely varies between individuals without quantifying it. A secondary snippet that the commonest MB cell type is almost twice as numerous in FlyWire as hemibrain is **unverified**.

## Fly-inspired ML borrows the motif, not the connectome, and the baselines are weak

The ML literature uses the abstract circuit motif (sparse random expansion, winner-take-all, partial updates), not connectome data, so it offers no test of connectome-as-prior. Dasgupta 2017 (ABS/SEC) framed the fly circuit as a locality-sensitive hashing variant; Ryali 2020 (ABS) claims learned BioHash codes outperform FlyHash's random projection; Kleyko & Rachkovskij 2025 (FT, results in figures only) found preprocessing and ranking choices mattered more than projection density and argue that expansion helps by adding capacity and nonlinear features ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14741v1)). The "learned beats random" claim thus belongs to hashing, from abstract-level evidence, and not to continual learning. No paper named "Liang et al." on fly hashing was identified.

For continual learning, the original FlyModel (Shen, Dasgupta, Navlakha) was compared only with EWC, GEM, BI-R, a vanilla net and an offline bound, on MNIST-20 and CIFAR-100; on MNIST-20 after five tasks it scored 0.86 against 0.77 for BI-R, and the authors state inputs are assumed pre-processed ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.07617)). There was **no SLDA or prototype/LDA baseline**. Fly-CL (Zou et al., ICLR 2026) on pretrained ViT/ResNet features compares against prompt/adapter methods and RanPAC; it loses to RanPAC on CIFAR-100 (93.89 vs 94.21), with small gains on CUB and VTAB, and the text read has no SLDA and no learned-vs-random ablation ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16877v2)). The notes found no independent reproduction or critique of FlyModel. A replay-free SLDA beating FlyModel is therefore plausible and uncontradicted, but it is the project's own empirical result, not a literature finding; any phrasing that FlyModel was benchmarked against strong baselines in its own paper would be wrong.

For "real wiring versus shuffled wiring", no study was found showing real MB wiring improves task performance over shuffled or random-sparse wiring. The nearest MB study, Zheng 2022, points the project's way. Reservoir studies on other connectomes are mixed and mostly secondary: a C. elegans benchmark reports real wiring winning on memory capacity but random controls winning on several other tasks (SEC, blog summary, **unverified** against the paper), and human-connectome and multi-species reservoir papers were read at abstract level only. The project's "never" is thus a statement about the project's own tasks. Two items cut the other way and should be acknowledged: Eichler 2017's abstract-level claim that single-PN KCs improve a larval model's discrimination, and the MacKenzie 2025 preprint (SEC) reporting PN-type biases that shape a learning hierarchy.

## Compartment valence is a first-order rule with documented exceptions

Aso 2014a/b describe 15 MB compartments tiled by 20 DAN types and 21 MBON types (34 MBONs per hemisphere). The canonical statement is that PPL1 DANs are punishment-signalling and PAM DANs reward-signalling, but the wording is hedged: Li 2020 says PPL1 DANs "generally convey punishment" and PAM DANs "generally convey reward" ([Li 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7909955)), and Aso 2014b names PPL1-gamma1pedc as the major shock signal and reward as carried by "a distributed set of PAM cluster DANs" ([Aso 2014b](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04580)). Aso 2014a itself does not state which family is which. The per-type evidence is uneven. It is strongest for PPL1-gamma1pedc, PPL1-gamma2alpha'1, PPL1-alpha3, PAM-alpha1 and PAM beta'2/gamma4 ([Aso & Rubin 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4987137); [Ichinose 2015](https://elifesciences.org/articles/10719) at abstract level). It is thin for PAM-beta1, beta2 and beta'1, and PPL1-alpha'2alpha2 shows only a synergistic effect.

The exceptions are published and specific. MB-M3, a PAM cell innervating beta2/beta'2a tips, induces aversive memory ([Aso 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3395599)). PAM-gamma3 gives aversive memory when activated, yet is needed for sugar reward, which it mediates by suppressing its own activity ([Yamagata 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5172549)); Li 2020 counts PAM12 (gamma3) among key aversive teaching signals. In a dopamine-null background, PPL1-gamma1pedc and PAM-gamma5/beta'2a produced memory of inverted valence, attributed to nitric oxide as an opposing cotransmitter ([Aso 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6948953)). PPL1 cells also serve appetitive extinction and reconsolidation ([Felsenberg 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5392358)), and aversive-memory extinction requires PAM DANs ([Felsenberg 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6198041)). The alpha'3 compartment encodes novelty/familiarity (Hattori 2017, ABS). The notes characterize most PPL1 "exceptions" as circuit context rather than a flipped dopamine sign, which matters for a depression-only simulator: PPL1-driven extinction is reproducible only if MBON->DAN feedback is wired.

MBON transmitter tracks valence in the original screen: glutamatergic MBONs repel when activated, cholinergic and GABAergic ones attract ([Aso 2014b](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04580)); transmitter counts by type are 7 glutamatergic, 4 GABAergic, 8 cholinergic, 2 undetermined ([Aso 2014a](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4273437)). The rule has explicit exceptions (for example GABAergic MBON-gamma1pedc>alpha/beta is required for aversive memory), and GABAergic MBONs split across clusters (gamma1pedc in PPL1, gamma3, gamma3beta'1 and beta'1 in PAM compartments), where the notes flag a disagreement between Aso 2014 and a Li 2020 summary. The project's MaleCNS tallies (15/20 inhibitory MBONs in PAM compartments; 19/28 cholinergic in PPL1 compartments) are directionally consistent with the literature but appear in no source and must be checked in the project data; also, "glutamate = inhibitory" is an assumption, not a fact (Li 2020, Owald 2015).

Plasticity timing is where the project's "forward/backward pairing reverses memory sign" claim is most exposed. The best-supported fact comes from full text: PPL1-gamma1pedc stimulation within 30 s after odour onset gives aversive memory, whereas stimulation 20-60 s **before** the odour gives appetitive memory ("relief learning") ([Aso & Rubin 2016](https://elifesciences.org/articles/16135); [Aso 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6948953)). Handler 2019 was read at **ABS only** in both notes: it reports that odour-dopamine associations can be written and reversed trial by trial depending on temporal relationship, with DopR1 and DopR2 directing depression or potentiation. Its time windows, the receptor-to-sign mapping and the backward-pairing outcome in a specific MBON were not verified. Hige 2015 (Neuron) was also ABS only: the abstract says plasticity "strictly depends on the temporal order", and the backward-pairing result for MBON-gamma1pedc>alpha/beta was not verified. A delta rule has no timing term and cannot by itself produce sign reversal, so any reversal in the project must come from an explicit timing term or eligibility trace.

## Prediction error and APL sparseness are weaker than the project assumes

Two direct blocking tests, one visual and one olfactory, found no blocking in flies. Brembs & Heisenberg 2001 (ABS) found compound stimuli gained equal associative strength even when one had been the better predictor, with only sensory preconditioning and a small second-order effect ([Regensburg record](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/28567)); Young et al. (ABS via database record; year and details unverified) reported that prior A+ training did not affect AB+ olfactory learning ([record](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn:443/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-21742045)). Zhao 2021 (FT) found an error-correcting predictive rule fit about 10x better in MSE than Hebbian, STDP and covariance rules, with 5 parameters, but on **28 data points**, one odour pair, aversive only, and the authors state "blocking has not been observed in flies" ([Zhao 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7990964/)). Felsenberg 2017 shows extinction versus reconsolidation depends on prediction accuracy, and Springer & Nawrot 2021 (ABS) is a model with MBON->DAN feedback reproducing extinction and "blocking-of-output", not new data. Eschbach 2020/2021 are larval, the second explicitly does not discuss reward prediction error, and the first (ABS) supports feedback motifs onto DANs. No study recording DAN activity as a prediction error in adult flies was retrieved, no olfactory overshadowing study in flies was found (a gap, not evidence of absence), and Cohn 2015, Riemensperger 2005 and Modi 2020 counter-evidence was not read. Aso & Rubin and Handler results (DAN activation alone writes a memory with sign set by timing) fit a teaching signal, not an error computation. A delta-rule model is defensible as "inspired by", not as "implements what fly DANs do".

On APL, Lin 2014 (FT, first ~100k of ~139k chars, figures not seen) is the key source. Blocking APL feedback impaired learned discrimination of a similar pair (IA:EB 4:1 vs 1:4) at 32 C but not 21 C, with temperature x APL interaction P = 0.0012 and a three-way ANOVA P = 0.009 ([Lin 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4000970/)). For dissimilar pairs, delta-DL vs IA:EB showed no difference (interaction P = 0.53) and OCT vs MCH showed P = 0.09 across groups (14-41 flies per group). Authors call the sparseness-behaviour link the "most plausible explanation", only 5-min memory was tested, only two dissimilar pairs, and single-APL labelling gave a non-significant marginal effect. The "20%" density in the project is a project parameter that Lin did not measure. Campbell 2013 (ABS) and Hige 2015 (ABS) support generalisation that rises with KC-pattern overlap, but no quantitative gradient was retrieved. Papadopoulou 2011 (ABS) is locust GGN feedback; Honegger 2011 and Stevens 2015 were not retrieved.

## Negative-result methods demand SESOI justification, multiplicity disclosure and tuning parity

Equivalence logic is well supported. Lakens 2017 (FULL) states that a non-significant test cannot support "no effect", that TOST needs both one-sided tests significant, that the 90% CI must lie within the bounds for alpha = .05, and that results fall into four categories (equivalent, different, different and equivalent, undetermined) ([Lakens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/)). The textbook chapter (FULL) adds that SESOI must be justified, TOST needs no correction for its two one-sided tests, a corrected alpha implies a CI of 100 - 2*alpha, and inconclusive results should be called inconclusive ([chapter](https://lakens.github.io/statistical_inferences/09-equivalencetest.html)). The notes' inference is that a three-outcome verdict collapses these four, so "no advantage" should be reported separately for equivalent versus inconclusive comparisons. A SESOI of 0.03 has no ML norm in the literature and must be justified internally; Bouthillier 2021 (FT) shows test-set sampling noise is often comparable to published gains, so the binomial SE at the actual test-set size should sit well below the 0.06-wide window ([Bouthillier 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.03098)). Single runs gave about 10% false positives and 75% false negatives in its simulations; Agarwal 2021 and Henderson 2018 (both FT) recommend bootstrap CIs and many seeds.

Multiplicity is the soft spot. The notes found no source prescribing family definition across stages of a staged study (alpha-investing and online FDR were searched once with no results), so per-stage families of 4-8 leave cross-study error uncontrolled. Their inference: because equivalence uses an intersection-union logic, inflation mainly threatens false "advantage" claims, the conservative direction for a "no advantage" paper; Holm is the safer FWER choice, and BH assumes independence that shared seeds and splits may violate. Re-testing a hypothesis in later stages after earlier results shaped the design is a forking-paths exposure (Gelman & Loken, search summaries only), and preregistration of later stages after seeing earlier data is only partially confirmatory (Nosek 2018, ABS).

Tuning parity is the main reviewer demand. Lucic 2018 (FT) found most GANs equal with enough tuning and that the range of the hyperparameter search changes conclusions (100 samples over wide ranges versus 50 over narrow); Musgrave 2020 (FT) found gains vanish under controlled protocols; Ferrari Dacrema 2019 (FT, partial) found 6 of 7 reproducible neural recommenders failed to beat tuned simple baselines ([Lucic](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.10337); [Musgrave](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.08505); [Dacrema](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.06902)). A null is only an equivalence finding if both arms had equal budgets and selected optima were not at grid edges. Negative-result venues exist (Karl 2024 position paper, ABS; ICBINB workshops via call-for-papers snippets).

On LLM review, same-model sub-agents are not independent. Kim 2025 (ABS) finds models from the same provider share errors, with correlation rising for larger models ([Kim 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)); Panickssery 2024 (FT) shows self-preference (GPT-4 recognised its own output 73.5% of the time; relabelling dropped self-preference from 0.73 to 0.32) ([Panickssery 2024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2404.13076)); Sharma 2023 (FT) shows sycophancy (accuracy fell up to 27% when a user challenged a correct answer); Zheng 2023 (FT) reports 85% human agreement, self-enhancement indicated but data deemed insufficient, and math errors falling from 14/20 to 3/20 with reference-guided prompting ([Zheng 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)). Kohli 2026 (search summary only, preprint) reports nine judges yielding about two effective votes. No study tested same-model sub-agents as reviewers of computational studies; the conclusion rests on cross-model correlation evidence. Liang 2024 (search summary only) supports LLM review as a pre-submission aid, not a substitute for independent review. ICMJE/COPE disclosure guidance came from secondary commentary only.

## Conclusion

The literature does not threaten the headline negative result; it changes what the project can say about why. Real wiring is not "random" in the strict sense, but the structure is small, class-specific and largely explained by bouton proximity, and the one model that tested it found random wiring best for generic discrimination, so a null on generic tasks is expected rather than surprising. The pivotal open point is task relevance: any advantage of real structure would come from routing through food-responsive core PNs, not from dimension, which implies the project's tasks and its teacher-student designs, not its wiring prior, determine whether a difference can appear.

The more vulnerable parts are the biological narratives wrapped around the null: conserved glomerular totals, universal PAM/PPL1 valence, a prediction-error dopamine, a clean APL "only" dissociation and a depth-limited Handler citation. Each can be rescued by narrowing, and the notes give the narrower wording. On method, the paper will be judged on whether the 0.03 SESOI sits above the test-set noise floor, whether baselines and the proposed method had equal tuning with grid-edge checks, how cross-stage multiplicity is disclosed, and whether same-model reviews are described as non-independent and backed by re-execution.
