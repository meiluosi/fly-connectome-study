# Fly-inspired machine learning and connectome-as-prior results

Reading-depth key: FT = full text (or large part) read via fetch tool, which returns a model-summarised view of the page and not raw text; ABS = abstract/landing page only; SEC = secondary source (search snippet, blog, press). Several PDFs came back as binary and could not be parsed; those are marked. No paper's tables were read verbatim except where noted. Treat numbers as "as reported by the fetch summary" unless marked.

## 1. FlyHash, BioHash and follow-ups; sparse expansion: what is claimed, and does random expansion alone explain it?

### Takeaway
FlyHash (sparse random expansion plus winner-take-all) is a data-independent LSH variant claimed to beat classical LSH. BioHash (learned expansion) is claimed to beat FlyHash and other hashing methods. A 2025 design-choice study finds preprocessing and ranking choices matter more than the expansion itself. The literature supports "learned/data-dependent beats random" for hashing, but I did not read the head-to-head tables, so I cannot quote effect sizes.

### Cited Findings
- Dasgupta, Stevens, Navlakha (2017), Science 358(6364):793-796, "A neural algorithm for a fundamental computing problem." The fly olfactory circuit is described as a variant of LSH using three departures from classical LSH, one of them an expansion to higher dimension with sparse coding. Read: ABS/SEC only (search snippets); no MAP numbers retrieved. — [CSHL repository](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/38630/); a PDF copy exists at [Caltech mirror](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Dasgupta-2017.pdf) but was not read.
- Sharma & Navlakha, "Improving similarity search with high-dimensional locality-sensitive hashing," arXiv:1812.01844 (Dec 2018). Claims data-independent LSH variants (including a multi-probe version) that outperform existing LSH methods for nearest-neighbour search on six benchmark datasets; the high-dimensional hash space is said to enhance separability. Read: ABS. No numbers, dataset names or baselines retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01844)
- Secondary report that Dasgupta-group follow-up work (DenseFly) finds a thresholded dense projection gives better separability than sparse FlyHash in high dimensions. Not verified against the original paper. Read: SEC (search summary). — [search summary of Kleyko-related literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14741)
- Ryali, Hopfield, Grinberg, Krotov (2020), ICML, "Bio-Inspired Hashing for Unsupervised Similarity Search," arXiv:2001.04907. FlyHash uses random projections and cannot learn from data. BioHash learns sparse high-dimensional codes with a local plasticity rule, and is claimed to outperform previously published hashing benchmarks. BioConvHash adds convolutions and improves further. Read: ABS. Datasets, baselines and numbers not retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04907)
- Kleyko & Rachkovskij (2025), "On Design Choices in Similarity-Preserving Sparse Randomized Embeddings," arXiv:2501.14741. Datasets SIFT (d=128), GLOVE (d=300), MNIST (d=784), N=10^4 each, MAP at K=200, 10 random projection draws. Preprocessing and similarity ranking had the largest effect. Projection density (1.56%, 10%, 50%) mattered little on SIFT/MNIST. Genetic per-column density optimisation gave no noticeable gain. The paper argues linear random projection alone cannot preserve similarity better than the raw vectors and that expansion helps by adding capacity and enabling nonlinear features. No numeric comparison against non-fly embeddings. Read: FT (HTML, summarised; results are in figures only). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14741v1)
- Litwin-Kumar, Harris, Axel, Sompolinsky, Abbott (2017), Neuron 93:1153-1164, "Optimal degrees of synaptic connectivity." Theory: the dimension of the Kenyon-cell (and cerebellar granule cell) representation peaks at a connectivity degree close to anatomical values; with random wiring, sparse connectivity (fewer than ten inputs per cell) can beat dense connectivity; dense wiring is favoured when synapses undergo supervised plasticity. Claw number "7" is not stated in the sources I saw. Read: ABS/SEC (search snippets and seminar abstracts). — [Neuron full text mirror](https://neurophysics.ucsd.edu/courses/physics_171/Optimal%20degrees%20of%20synaptic%20connectivity.pdf) (not read), [HUJI listing](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/optimal-degrees-of-synaptic-connectivity/)
- No paper called "Liang et al." on fly hashing was found by my search; I could not identify that reference. Treat as unverified.

### Inferences
- Consistent with the project (a)/(b): the literature itself treats random sparse expansion as a baseline that learned expansion (BioHash) improves on, so a "real wiring does not beat random-sparse" result is not surprising. The data-dependent-beats-random claim comes from hashing, not from connectome wiring.
- Kleyko & Rachkovskij suggest FlyHash performance is dominated by preprocessing and ranking choices, which supports caution about attributing benefit to expansion per se.

### Gaps
- Exact MAP/accuracy tables for FlyHash, DenseFly and BioHash vs. baselines (full-text tables not read).
- "Liang et al." reference unidentified.
- A clean controlled test of sparse expansion vs. dense random projection vs. PCA at matched dimension was not found.

## 2. Fly-inspired continual learning: FlyModel, Fly-CL, critiques

### Takeaway
The original FlyModel (Shen, Dasgupta, Navlakha) was evaluated on only two class-incremental benchmarks against EWC, GEM, BI-R and a vanilla net, with no SLDA-type or pretrained-feature prefix baselines. The 2025-26 Zou et al. papers (a plug-in "Fly Model" and Fly-CL) work on pretrained-backbone features and compare to prompt/adapter/RanPAC-type baselines. None of what I read compares to SLDA. I found no independent reproduction or critique paper of FlyModel.

### Cited Findings
- Shen, Dasgupta, Navlakha, "Algorithmic insights on continual learning from fruit flies," arXiv:2107.07617 (2021); journal version "Reducing Catastrophic Forgetting With Associative Learning: A Lesson From Fruit Flies," Neural Computation 2023. Read: FT of the ar5iv HTML (summarised); the arXiv PDF was binary and unreadable.
  - Datasets: MNIST-20 (MNIST plus Fashion-MNIST relabelled to 20 classes, 10 two-class tasks) and CIFAR-100 (25 tasks of 4 classes). Class-incremental, one pass per task.
  - Baselines: EWC, GEM, BI-R (generative replay), vanilla (lower bound), offline (upper bound). SI is mentioned in related work but not compared.
  - MNIST-20 after 5 tasks (10 classes): FlyModel 0.86 ± 0.0006; BI-R 0.77 ± 0.02; GEM 0.69 ± 0.02; EWC 0.58 ± 0.10; vanilla 0.19. After 10 tasks FlyModel is at least 0.19 above every other continual method and 0.11 below offline. CIFAR-100: at least 0.15 above all continual methods, 0.13 below offline.
  - Ablations (MNIST-20): sparse coding about 0.64 vs. dense 0.07; partial freezing vs. logistic regression on sparse codes 0.75 vs. 0.54 (MNIST-20), 0.41 vs. 0.21 (CIFAR-100).
  - Two perceptron changes: update the correct-class weights even when the prediction is right; never decrease wrong-class weights on a mistake.
  - Self-stated limitations: only two datasets; inputs assumed already pre-processed (e.g., pretrained network); forgetting term zero; hyperparameters by grid search, five seeds; paper itself is inconsistent about "three" vs. four compared methods; no recurrence, no compartments, no dopamine weight-decrease.
  — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.07617), [search summary](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07617), [CSHL record](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/41202/)
- Zou, Zang, Ji (2025), "Structural features of the fly olfactory circuit mitigate the stability-plasticity dilemma in continual learning," arXiv:2502.01427. A "Fly Model" plug-and-play component combined with modern CL methods; claims improved stability and plasticity at minimal cost. Read: ABS only (HTML 404). Baselines, benchmarks, numbers not retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01427)
- Zou et al., "Fly-CL," arXiv:2510.16877, ICLR 2026 (Tsinghua / Tianjin). Pipeline: normalise pretrained features, high-dimensional sparse random projection with top-k, streaming ridge classifier, prototype matching. Read: FT of the first 100k of 174k characters of the HTML (summarised); appendix limitations not read.
  - Datasets: CIFAR-100, CUB-200-2011, VTAB (main tables); ImageNet-R/A also mentioned. Backbones ViT-B/16 and ResNet-50.
  - Baselines: L2P, DualPrompt, InfLoRA, SEMA, MoE-Adapter, EASE, RanPAC, F-OAL. SLDA not mentioned in the text I read.
  - ViT-B/16 average accuracy (Table 1 as relayed): Fly-CL 93.89 / 93.84 / 96.54 (CIFAR-100 / CUB / VTAB); RanPAC 94.21 / 92.67 / 94.16; EASE 92.96 / 89.56 / 94.02; F-OAL 91.96 / 91.13 / 94.68. So Fly-CL is not best on CIFAR-100 (RanPAC higher by 0.32). Training time (s) CIFAR-100: Fly-CL 19.07 vs. RanPAC 98.62, EASE 621.26.
  - ResNet-50: Fly-CL 84.61 / 80.25 / 94.00 vs. RanPAC 82.72 / 78.72 / 92.80.
  - Hyperparameter defaults: m=10,000 expansion, p=300 synapses/KC, k=3,000 active. Accuracy saturates beyond m≈10,000; plateau over k.
  - No learned-vs-random projection ablation in the text read; the projection is fixed and random. RanPAC (a closely related random-projection plus ridge approach) is the nearest baseline, so the gain over generic random projection is small (CIFAR-100 lost; +1.2 CUB, +2.4 VTAB).
  — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16877v2), [ICLR poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007883)
- Not found: any independent reproduction of FlyModel, or any paper explicitly criticising the fly-CL literature for weak baselines or the replay-free SLDA comparison. Search returned none. The criticisms in your brief are therefore my inference, not source-supported claims.

### Inferences
- Consistent with project (a): the original FlyModel's baselines (EWC, GEM, BI-R, vanilla) are weak for class-incremental learning compared with prototype/LDA methods on fixed features; the paper itself says inputs are assumed pre-processed. A replay-free SLDA beating FlyModel on three benchmarks is plausible and not contradicted by anything I read. It is also consistent with Fly-CL, whose best baselines are RanPAC-type random-projection-plus-ridge methods that are close cousins of SLDA-style streaming statistics.
- Fly-CL's gains over RanPAC are small and mixed, which supports "random expansion with a good readout is most of the effect."

### Gaps
- Zou 2502.01427 experimental sections (HTML failed). Fly-CL appendix and Figure 4 ablations. Any SLDA comparison in either.
- Whether later papers (post-2023) re-ran FlyModel; searched once only.

## 3. Does real connectome wiring help computation? Real vs. shuffled / null

### Takeaway
I found no study showing that real mushroom-body wiring improves task performance over shuffled or random-sparse wiring. The nearest MB study (Zheng et al.) found the observed structure performed worse than random wiring unless input came through specific PN types. Reservoir studies on other connectomes give task-dependent and mixed results, with real wiring winning mostly on memory capacity.

### Cited Findings
- Zheng et al. (2022), Current Biology 32:3334-3349, "Structured sampling of olfactory input by the fly mushroom body." Whole-brain EM; food-responsive PN types converge on KCs above chance and co-arborise, preferentially onto αβ and α'β' KCs. In the feed-forward model (10 odors, 2 categories, 2,200 KCs, max-margin readout, 100 networks x 10,000 trials) the random-glomerulus model discriminated best; more realistic models had higher error when all 51 PN types provided input, with performance recovered when activity went only through the 19 "community" PN types (or food-responsive PNs). Caveats: no recurrence or APL, 844 imputed KCs, KC thresholding removed, error values only in figures, authors call the interpretation speculative. Read: FT of first 100k of 180k characters (PMC, summarised). — [PMC9413950](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9413950), [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.04.17.047167.full.pdf)
- MacKenzie et al. (2025), bioRxiv, "Connectivity biases generate a learning hierarchy in the Drosophila mushroom body." Reported per search summary: PN-to-KC connectivity is biased by PN type; odors activating more than 20% of KCs support more robust associative memory, and those activating fewer than 10% are poorly learned; VL1 PNs are an exception. Read: SEC only (fetch returned 403). Preprint. Contains behavioural/imaging correlations, not a shuffled-connectome model comparison as far as I can tell. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.29.684686.full.pdf)
- Lappalainen et al. (2024), Nature 634:1132-1140, "Connectome-constrained networks predict neural activity across the fly visual system." Optic lobe motion pathway, 64 cell types; connectivity fixed from EM, unknown single-neuron/synapse parameters fit by deep learning for motion detection; predictions agreed with measurements from 26 studies. This is connectome-as-constraint for explaining activity in a visual circuit, not a demonstration that real wiring beats randomised wiring on a task. Read: SEC (abstract-level search results). — [Max Planck record](https://is.mpg.de/publications/lappalainennature24), [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v634y2024i8036d10.1038_s41586-024-07939-3.html)
- Suárez, Richards, Lajoie, Misic (2021), Nature Machine Intelligence, "Learning function from structure in neuromorphic networks." Human diffusion-MRI connectomes as reservoirs on a memory task; performance highest for biologically realistic architectures in a critical regime; modularity matters. Null-model details and effect sizes not retrieved. Read: ABS/SEC. Human, not fly. — [bioRxiv 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.11.10.350876.full.pdf) (403 on fetch), [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-artificial-neural-networks-real-brains.html)
- d'Andrea, Puppin, De Domenico (2022), arXiv:2211.00161, "Complex topological features of reservoirs shape learning performances in bio-inspired recurrent neural networks." 45 empirical connectomes from 8 species vs. synthetic networks; performance correlates with node count and rank of the activation covariance; modularity and link density matter beyond simple topological models. The abstract gives no real-vs-null effect sizes; the PDF was unreadable. Read: ABS. (A search summary says a related arXiv study used ER, configuration and SBM nulls; I could not confirm which paper.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00161)
- Reimers, Ramstad, Hubin, Nichele (2026), "Benchmarking the Connectomes of Caenorhabditis elegans within the Reservoir Computing Framework" (cited as arXiv:2609.30508 by the blog; ID unverified). 22 C. elegans maps, 180 neurons, nulls with rewired wiring, random I/O, or both, 15 draws. Real wiring beat controls only on memory capacity (beta-regression contrast +0.305 / +0.657 / +0.951 for wiring / I/O / both randomised); random controls won on the Hénon map (-1.342 / -1.086 / -1.426), mostly on Mackey-Glass and Go/No-go; perceptual decision made no clear difference. Memory-capacity advantage vanished with contact-area maps and with RMSE scoring. Read: SEC only (Pebblous blog summary), numbers unverified against the paper. — [blog](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/celegans-connectome-reservoir-benchmark/en/)
- Search-level snippets only (not read): Ulster poster "Random reservoirs rule" arguing simple topologies often match complex ones on prediction ([Ulster](https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/random-and-biological-network-connectivity-for-reservoir-computin/)); COSYNE 2025 abstract that connectome reservoirs have fewer, more task-selective units ([COSYNE](https://world-wide.org/conf/cosyne-25/sparse-neural-engagement-connectome-h7r1dty6)). The names Eschbach, Warren, Endo, Morra 2023 and Damicelli 2022 were not located or read.

### Inferences
- Consistent with project (b): no source contradicts "real MB wiring never beat shuffled/random-sparse." Zheng et al. is the closest and actually points the same way for generic discrimination.
- Consistent with (c) in spirit: where real wiring wins in reservoir work it is on specific metrics (memory capacity, which is itself fragile to scoring), and the project's teacher-student pattern (difference only when labels derive from the real wiring) fits the idea that any advantage is tied to task structure. No source directly tests teacher-student designs, so this match is an inference.
- Lappalainen et al. is not evidence for performance benefits of wiring; it is evidence that wiring plus fitted parameters explains activity.

### Gaps
- Full-text null-model results for Suárez 2021, d'Andrea 2022, Morra 2023, Damicelli 2022.
- Eschbach 2020/2021, Warren, Endo MB papers (not located). Any paper doing shuffled-vs-real hemibrain/FlyWire MB models on a learning task.
- Verification of Reimers et al. from the primary paper.

## Project statements and literature: flags

- No project statement (a), (b) or (c) is contradicted by anything I read. Caveat: absence of contrary results reflects a limited search (about 20 tool calls, mostly summaries), not proof that none exist.
- Possible tension to check in the project text: any claim that "the literature shows real MB wiring is random-like" would be too strong. Zheng et al. 2022 and MacKenzie et al. 2025 report non-random PN-KC structure (food-responsive PN overconvergence; PN-type biases). The project's null result is about performance, which is a different claim than randomness of wiring. Any phrasing that the fly-CL literature "claims" connectome-derived advantages should note that FlyModel and Fly-CL use only the abstract motif (expansion, sparsity, partial update) and not connectome data.
- Any statement that FlyModel was compared with SLDA or strong baselines in its own paper would be wrong: the paper used EWC, GEM, BI-R, vanilla, offline.
- Any statement that "learned expansion beats random" should be attributed to hashing work (BioHash, from its abstract) and not to continual learning. Fly-CL's text I read has no learned-vs-random ablation.
