## (a) Do fly dopamine neurons and learning follow Rescorla-Wagner-like prediction-error rules?

### Takeaway
There is circuit evidence for DAN-involving feedback and for "prediction-accuracy-dependent" extinction vs reconsolidation (Felsenberg 2017), plus one behavioural model-fit paper (Zhao 2021) that favours an error-correcting rule. The only direct blocking tests I found (Brembs & Heisenberg 2001 visual; Young et al. olfactory) found NO blocking. Zhao itself says blocking has not been observed in flies. "Dopamine signals prediction error" is therefore a hypothesis with partial support, not an established fact.

### Cited Findings
- Brembs & Heisenberg 2001, J Exp Biol 204(16):2849-2859 (ABS). In flight-simulator conditioning with pattern plus colour compounds, both stimuli gained equal associative strength even when one had previously been the better predictor. The authors conclude there is no strong blocking. They did find sensory preconditioning and a small second-order conditioning effect. — [Regensburg repository summary](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/28567)
- Young, Wessnitzer, Armstrong & Webb (year unconfirmed; the search results list a 2011 companion model paper, "Elemental and non-elemental olfactory learning in Drosophila", Neurobiol Learn Mem — citation details need verification) (ABS via database record). Olfactory AB+ training was not affected by prior A+ training, so flies "do not exhibit blocking in this task". Flies learned positive patterning but not negative patterning or biconditional discrimination. None of the standard learning models fit fully. — [OpenLB record](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn:443/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-21742045), [search overview](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn:443/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-21698405). Later: "Fruit flies can learn non-elemental olfactory discriminations" (Proc R Soc B) reports that flies CAN solve negative patterning, contradicting the earlier null. — [PMC7735272](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7735272)
- Overshadowing: I found no Drosophila olfactory overshadowing study in this search. Overshadowing literature found was in honeybees only. This is a gap, not evidence of absence. — search results (no URL for a fly study)
- Zhao et al. 2021, Sci Rep 11:6795, doi 10.1038/s41598-021-85841-y (FT, first ~80% of text). Canton-S groups of 60-100 flies, benzaldehyde and limonene, shock-paired odour with varying pulse number, timing and strength. They report 28 data points in all. An error-correcting predictive rule (prediction error = internal shock representation minus odour-evoked value; 5 parameters, MSE 6.4e-4) fit about 10x better in MSE than Hebbian, STDP and covariance rules. Akaike relative likelihoods of the alternatives were roughly 1e-7 to 1e-19. The authors state their rule is related to Rescorla-Wagner but is continuous-time. They state that "blocking has not been observed in flies" and propose that compound odours may recruit different MBON groups. Extinction is predicted as unlearning. Second-order conditioning is not addressed. Their circuit implementations (DANs encode the error, or DANs encode a target) are proposals and the target-driven version is untested. Limitations: small dataset, one odour pair, aversive only. Sign reversal, valence and novelty are discussed only as outlook. — [Zhao 2021 PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7990964/)
- Felsenberg et al. 2017, Nature 544:240-244, doi 10.1038/nature21716 (ABS). Reactivating a reward memory gives extinction or reconsolidation depending on prediction accuracy at recall. Extinction needs MBONs that drive negatively reinforcing DANs and builds a parallel aversive memory. Reconsolidation needs gamma2alpha'1 MBONs that re-engage positively reinforcing DANs. — [Oxford Waddell lab record](https://www.dpag.ox.ac.uk/publications/688004), [PMC5392358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5392358)
- Felsenberg 2018 (not located). From memory: Felsenberg et al., Cell 2018, "Integration of parallel opposing memories underlies memory extinction". Only secondary summaries (talk abstract, DFG report) were found: extinction leaves the original memory and a parallel extinction memory co-existing. MEM, unverified. — [DFG project report](https://gepris.dfg.de/gepris/projekt/277848606/ergebnisse)
- Eschbach et al. 2020, Nat Neurosci 23:544-555, doi 10.1038/s41593-020-0607-9 (ABS). This is LARVAL Drosophila, not adult. A synaptic-resolution connectome of circuits upstream of all DANs; DANs receive feedback from MBON/aversive and appetitive systems. Modelling shows these feedback motifs improve flexibility and learning-task performance. — [PubMed 32203499 via search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32203499/), [PMC7145459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7145459)
- Eschbach et al. 2021, eLife 10:e62567 (FT, first ~1/3). Also LARVA. Maps neurons downstream of MBONs and convergence neurons that combine MBON (learned) and lateral horn (innate) valence. The paper does NOT discuss reward prediction error. MB2ONs and convergence neurons feed back onto DANs (40 of 167 MB2ONs synapse directly onto modulatory neurons). The model is conceptual, not computational. — [eLife 62567](https://elifesciences.org/articles/62567)
- Jacob & Waddell 2020, Neuron 106(6):977-991 (ABS; erratum Neuron 110:2703, 2022). After spaced aversive training flies form an aversive memory for the CS+ and a slower "safety memory" for the CS-, which depends on rewarding DANs and on trial repetition, order and spacing. The memories sit in different MBON combinations. — [PMC7302427](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7302427/), [erratum PMC9394686](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9394686/)
- Springer & Nawrot 2021, eNeuro 8(3), doi 10.1523/ENEURO.0549-20.2021 (ABS). A theoretical model: plastic KC to MBON synapses, separate mutually inhibiting appetitive and aversive MBON pathways, and an MBON to DAN feedback loop acting as a simple reward-prediction mechanism. It reproduces published learning, extinction and blocking-of-output experiments. It is a model, not new data. — [eNeuro abstract](https://www.eneuro.org/content/8/3/ENEURO.0549-20.2021.abstract)
- Handler et al. 2019, Cell 178:60-75, doi 10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.040 (ABS). Odour preference can be written and reversed trial by trial depending on the timing of odour relative to DAN activation. Reversals track bidirectional plasticity in the MB. DopR1 and DopR2 couple to different second messengers (depression vs potentiation) and set the temporal sensitivity. — [Cornell record](https://teach.weill.cornell.edu/mdphd/publication/distinct-dopamine-receptor-pathways-underlie-temporal-sensitivity-associative-learning), [PMC9012144](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9012144)
- Aso & Rubin 2016, eLife 5:e16135 (ABS). Optogenetic activation of individual DAN types: memories differ across compartments in training requirements, decay, capacity and flexibility. One DAN type can write or reduce an aversive memory, or write an appetitive memory, depending on timing. The authors conclude different learning rules run in parallel memory systems. — [eLife 16135](https://elifesciences.org/articles/16135)
- Hige et al. 2015, Neuron (ABS). DAN activation paired with odour depresses KC to MBON synapses, only when the odour precedes the DAN activation. Compartment-specific. Overlap in sparse KC representations sets plasticity specificity and generalization. — [PMC4674068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4674068)
- Modi, Shuai & Turner 2020, Annu Rev Neurosci — NOT retrieved (MEM only: a review of MB circuit function and dopamine-gated plasticity).
- Counter-evidence that DANs report "US value / valence" rather than an error: no direct paper was retrieved in this session. The Aso & Rubin and Handler results (DAN activation alone writes a memory, with a sign set by timing) are consistent with DANs acting as a teaching signal. They are not evidence for error computation. Specific counter-papers I recall but did not verify (MEM): Cohn, Morantte & Ruta 2015 Cell (DAN-based coincidence detection and MBON-to-DAN circuits); Riemensperger 2005 (DANs sufficient as the US).

### Inferences
- "Fly learning is Rescorla-Wagner" is not supported by blocking data. Both direct blocking tests found none (one visual, one olfactory). Zhao 2021 and Springer & Nawrot 2021 both explain this by arguing odours recruit separate MBON sets, which is an auxiliary assumption, not evidence for the delta rule.
- Evidence consistent with an error-like rule: extinction vs reconsolidation depends on prediction accuracy (Felsenberg 2017); MBON to DAN feedback exists (larva: Eschbach 2020/2021; adult: Felsenberg 2017); Zhao 2021 fits to shock timing and strength. All of it is indirect or phenomenological, and the Zhao dataset is small.
- A delta-rule project is defensible as "a model inspired by", and not as "implements what fly DANs do".
- Handler 2019 reversal is a timing (STDP-like, receptor-dependent) rule driven by DAN-odour order. A delta rule driven by R minus predicted valence has no timing dependence of its own. Reproducing forward/backward sign reversal needs an explicit timing term or an eligibility trace. A delta rule alone cannot account for it.

### Gaps
- No Drosophila olfactory overshadowing study found. Full text of Young et al. and the Brembs paper was not read. No second-order olfactory conditioning study retrieved. Felsenberg 2018 and Modi 2020 not retrieved. No retrieved paper directly recording DAN activity as a prediction error in adult flies.

## (b) KC sparseness, APL feedback inhibition, odour discrimination

### Takeaway
Lin 2014: blocking APL feedback (shibire-ts) impaired learned discrimination of the SIMILAR odour pair but not the tested dissimilar pairs, at the 5-min memory timescale. However, the dissimilar OCT vs MCH comparison had P = 0.09 (not significant, but not far off), and the authors say the sparseness-behaviour link is the "most plausible explanation", not proven. KC fraction is ~5-10% per odour (Lin's intro); the "~5%" figure is common (Stevens 2015 and others, not retrieved).

### Cited Findings
- Lin, Bygrave, de Calignon, Lee & Miesenböck 2014, Nat Neurosci 17:559-568, doi 10.1038/nn.3660 (FT, first 100k of 139k chars; summary relayed). Abstract: KC to APL feedback loop controls sparseness; disrupting it reduced sparseness, raised odour correlations, and prevented learning to discriminate similar but not dissimilar odours. — [PMC4000970](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4000970/), [White Rose record](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/89890)
  - Similar pair: IA:EB 4:1 vs 1:4 mixtures (modelled ORN correlation r = 0.47 and 0.55). APL-blocked flies were impaired at 32 C but not 21 C, temperature x APL interaction P = 0.0012; three-way ANOVA (odour pair x temp x APL) P = 0.009; 16-55 flies per group. Single-APL labelling gave a marginal, non-significant impairment. In the relayed text the temperature manipulation is described as blocking APL with shibire-ts.
  - Dissimilar pairs: delta-DL vs IA:EB 4:1, no difference at 21 or 32 C, interaction P = 0.53. OCT vs MCH, no significant difference, P = 0.09 across all groups, 14-41 per group. Untrained preference did not differ, P = 0.14.
  - Imaging: sparseness and correlation changes are reported as P < 0.001 in the figures (Figs. 5, 6, 8). The text gives no numeric KC fraction under APL manipulation. The fetch summary reports no standardised effect sizes (no Cohen's d) and no learning-score values; those are in the figures only. I did not see the figures.
  - Authors' caveats (as relayed): the null on the dissimilar pair argues against a general learning defect. The mechanistic link is "most plausible", not proven. Similarity is defined by their own correlation analysis. Tested at 5-min memory only. One blocking method with stochastic APL labelling. Only two dissimilar pairs tested.
- Campbell et al. 2013, J Neurosci 33(25):10568-10581, doi 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0682-12.2013 (ABS; the task brief says Neuron, which appears wrong). Two-photon imaging of >100 KCs. Ensembles of as few as 25 KCs matched behavioural discrimination accuracy. Generalization of an aversive association to novel odours depended on the similarity of the evoked MB activity patterns. — [PMC3685844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3685844), [CSHL record](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/28391/)
- Papadopoulou, Cassenaer, Nowotny & Laurent 2011, Science 332:721-725 (ABS). In locust, a single non-spiking GABAergic giant interneuron (GGN) forms a normalising negative-feedback loop that keeps KC output sparse over a wide range of input strengths. — [Caltech record](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20110519-104644303)
- Hige 2015 (see above, ABS): overlap of sparse KC representations sets how specific the plasticity is and how far learning generalizes.
- Differential vs absolute training: a later study found differential conditioning reduces behavioural generalization to chemically similar odours and decorrelates MB representations; antennal-lobe inhibition was needed for fine discrimination (ABS via search overview, paper not identified). — [search overview](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6827587)
- Honegger, Campbell & Turner 2011 and Stevens 2015: not retrieved. MEM: Honegger 2011 (J Neurosci) reports APL and MB-aligned cellular recordings showing sparse KC responses; Stevens 2015 (PNAS) argues sparseness near 5-10% is optimal-ish for the number of KCs. Unverified.

### Inferences
- "APL loss reduces valence separation between similar odours more than between dissimilar ones" matches the qualitative pattern in Lin 2014 behaviour. It is NOT a quantitative match: Lin's evidence is a significant similar-pair interaction plus non-significant dissimilar pairs (one at P = 0.09). Absence of significance is not proof that dissimilar discrimination was spared.
- Lin's manipulation removes feedback inhibition. Their reported effect is on correlations and sparseness measured by imaging, not on a fixed "5% to 20%" density; the 20% number is a project parameter, not something the paper measured (I saw no such figure in the relayed text).
- Lin's behavioural readout is the discrimination of CS+ from CS- (learning performance score), not "valence separation" in a model's units.

### Gaps
- Did not read Lin's figures, so I cannot give per-group learning scores or Cohen's d. Honegger 2011 and Stevens 2015 not retrieved; the exact "~5%" attribution is unconfirmed.

## (c) Generalization gradients with odour similarity

### Takeaway
Campbell 2013 and Hige 2015 support that generalization is a function of KC-pattern overlap. I found no paper reporting a graded generalization curve versus an explicit similarity axis beyond Campbell's blends.

### Cited Findings
- Campbell 2013 (ABS): generalization scales with overlap of the evoked MB activity patterns; the two behaviours are explained within one framework. — [PMC3685844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3685844)
- Hige 2015 (ABS): overlap in sparse KC representations determines specificity and generalization of plasticity. — [PMC4674068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4674068)

### Inferences
- "Generalisation falls with overlap" is supported in direction. Whether it is monotone or sharply thresholded in flies is not established by what I read.

### Gaps
- No quantitative gradient (effect vs correlation) retrieved.

## Claims in the project that are stronger than the literature

1. "Dopamine signals prediction error" — stronger than evidence. Supported by feedback circuits and one model fit (Zhao 2021, 28 data points), contradicted in spirit by repeated no-blocking results (Brembs 2001; Young et al.). Eschbach 2020/2021 are larva and do not claim RPE. Suggested wording: "the model uses an error-driven update as a hypothesis".
2. "Consistent with Lin 2014" — qualitatively fair. Dissimilar discrimination was spared in the sense of non-significant tests (interaction P = 0.53 for delta-DL vs IA:EB; P = 0.09 for OCT vs MCH), only at 5-min memory, only two dissimilar pairs, partly stochastic APL labelling. The effect was a similar-pair impairment at 32 C, interaction P = 0.0012.
3. "Generalisation falls with overlap" — direction supported (Campbell 2013, Hige 2015). Functional form unknown.
4. Forward/backward sign reversal (Handler 2019): not a property of a delta rule; needs an explicit timing term, and it depends on DopR1/DopR2 rather than on the error.
