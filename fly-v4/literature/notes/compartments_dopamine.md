## 1. Compartment list and which DAN cluster (PAM vs PPL1) innervates which; stated valence of each cluster

### Takeaway
The MB lobes have 15 compartments tiled by 20 DAN types and 21 MBON types (34 MBONs/hemisphere). The canonical scheme (Aso 2014a/b; restated by Li 2020 hemibrain) is PPL1 = punishment (vertical-lobe/γ1-2 compartments: γ1, γ2, α'1, α'2, α'3, α2, α3, plus pedc), PAM = reward (horizontal-lobe compartments: β1, β2, β'1, β'2, γ3, γ4, γ5, and α1). Aso's own wording is hedged ("generally", "likely", "thought to"); the strongest valence evidence is for PPL1-γ1pedc (shock) and for a *distributed set* of PAM DANs (sugar), not for every individual cell type.

### Cited Findings
- Compartment count/tiling: MBONs "form 15 compartments that collectively tile the MB lobes"; each DAN type "projects axons to one, or at most two" compartments. — Aso et al. 2014a, eLife 3:e04577, PMID 25535793, [eLife page](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04577) [FT-partial; abstract wording quoted by tool]
- 17 of 20 DAN types project to a single compartment; 3 DAN types innervate more than one lobe compartment (e.g. the PAM type projecting to γ4 extends dendrites to γ1 and γ2; PPL1-γ1pedc covers γ1 plus the distal-peduncle core). — Aso 2014a, [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04577) [FT-partial]
- Cluster-to-compartment mapping (from the cell-type names in Aso 2014a Table 1, as paraphrased by the fetch tool, and consistent with names used in Aso & Rubin 2016, Felsenberg 2017/2018, Cervantes-Sandoval 2017): PAM = α1, β1, β2, β'1, β'2, γ3, γ4, γ5 (the paper only names a "β'2" family, PAM-β'2a, etc.). PPL1 = γ1(+pedc), γ2+α'1 (one DAN, PPL1-γ2α'1/MB-MV1), α'2+α2 (PPL1-α'2α2/MB-V1), α3, α'3. The Table 1 list was NOT read directly line by line; mapping from tool paraphrase. — Aso 2014a [FT-partial]; Cervantes-Sandoval 2017 confirms "PPL1 ... the set of DAn that innervate the vertical lobes, heel, and junction area of the MB (compartments γ1–2, α1–3, α'1–3)" — note this sentence lists α1 under PPL1, which conflicts with the PAM-α1 naming in Ichinose 2015 / Aso 2014a (a loose statement in that paper; PAM-α1 is the established α1 DAN) — [Cervantes-Sandoval 2017, eLife 6:e23789](https://elifesciences.org/articles/23789), PMID 28489528 [FT]
- Valence statement (Aso 2014b): "Activation of subsets of DANs can convey punishment or reward, respectively"; "PPL1-γ1pedc DANs play a major role to mediate punishment signals" with a minor contribution from PPL1-γ2α'1 (Figure 14 legend); "reward signals are mediated by a distributed set of PAM cluster DANs" that innervate the compartments of glutamatergic MBONs; the cholinergic-MBON compartments "do not receive inputs from reward-mediating PAM cluster DANs, but instead from PPL1 cluster DANs." — Aso et al. 2014b, eLife 3:e04580, PMID 25535794, [eLife page](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04580) and Europe PMC full text PMC4273436 [FT: text of PMC4273436 searched; the discussion quotes were from the eLife page fetch, FT-partial]
- Aso 2014b also: PPL1-γ1pedc "likely convey the electric shock punishment signal"; PAM neurons innervating the β'2-associated compartments are "thought to convey the sucrose reward signal"; PPL1 DANs "shown to be dispensable for odor-sugar memory." — [Aso 2014b](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04580) [FT]
- Aso 2014a does NOT itself state which family is reward vs punishment (tool-checked): it says only that DANs "fall into two families, PAM and PPL1" and that different DAN types respond to USs of different valence, deferring to the companion paper's Figure 1A. — [Aso 2014a](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04577) [FT-partial]
- Li et al. 2020 (hemibrain MB connectome) restates the rule with hedging: "PPL1 DANs innervate the vertical lobes and generally convey punishment, whereas PAM DANs innervate horizontal lobes and generally convey reward"; "DANs are divided into two major groups, PPL1 and PAM, that preferentially encode punishment and reward." Hemibrain has 5 PPL1 DAN types and 150 PAM DANs; 99 of 150 PAM and all five PPL1 receive direct MBON input. — Li et al. 2020, eLife 9:e62576, [PMC7909955](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7909955) [FT]
- Strongest per-type punishment evidence: Aso & Rubin 2016 reversed the preference-index sign for PPL1-γ1pedc (MB320C), PPL1-γ2α'1+PPL1-α'2α2 (MB099C) and PPL1-α3 (MB630B) because activation produced aversive memory; PPL1-α'2α2 alone "does not respond to heat and shock and cannot induce aversive memory by itself, but can have a synergistic effect ... when coactivated with PPL1-γ2α'1" (Aso et al. 2019). — Aso & Rubin 2016, eLife 5:e16135, [PMC4987137](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4987137), PMID 27441388 [FT]; Aso et al. 2019, eLife 8:e49257, [PMC6948953](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6948953) [FT]
- Strongest per-type reward evidence: PAM-α1 (Ichinose 2015, "single class of reward-signaling dopamine neurons (PAM-α1)", eLife 4:e10719, PMID 26573957 [ABS; PMC4643015 OA XML not retrievable]); Yamagata 2015 PNAS: two distinct DAN subsets signal reward for STM vs LTM, "a single type of dopamine neuron" mediates LTM reward [ABS] (Aso&Rubin 2016 text identifies the LTM one with α1 in connection to the same literature [FT]); Huetteroth 2015 Curr Biol: OAMB-expressing DANs projecting to β'2 and γ4 convey sweet-taste STM reward; γ5b DANs required for nutrient LTM, β lobe and adjacent α1 DANs can artificially reinforce LTM [ABS], PMID 25728694.
- Owald 2015 (Neuron 86:417-427, PMID 25864636): "Dopaminergic neurons in the PAM cluster ... convey rewarding reinforcement"; the 0104-GAL4 PAM DANs "densely innervate the β' and γ lobe tips." [FT]
- Felsenberg 2017 (Nature 544:240; PMID 28379939): "Aversive DANs overlap with the dendrites of MBONs directing behavioral approach whereas rewarding DANs overlay the dendrites of MBONs driving avoidance"; the PPL1 cluster is called "aversive DANs" and PAM "rewarding DANs." — [PMC5392358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5392358) [FT]

### Inferences
- The PAM = reward / PPL1 = punishment partition is a good first-order description, but the literature authors themselves qualify it ("generally", "preferentially"); a project hard-coding it by cluster is consistent with the canonical statement, but the compartment-level exceptions in section 2 are real and published.
- Treat per-type claims as differing in evidential weight: PPL1-γ1pedc, PPL1-γ2α'1, PPL1-α3, PAM-α1, PAM β'2/γ4/γ5 have direct artificial-activation evidence; PPL1-α'2α2 only has "synergistic" evidence; PPL1-α'3 is a novelty/familiarity compartment (see section 2); PAM-β1/β2/β'1 valence rests largely on sparse or secondary evidence in what I read.

- I did not read Aso 2014a Table 1 / Figure 1A of 2014b directly (page truncation, figures not text); a definitive per-compartment cluster table should be re-checked against those figures.
- No per-compartment MBON counts were retrievable as text.

## 2. Exceptions: aversive PAM DANs, non-aversive PPL1 DANs, other roles

### Takeaway
Documented exceptions: (a) PAM cells that carry aversive signals (MB-M3 in β2/β'2a, PAM-γ3, and valence-inverted/mixed PAM-γ5/β'2a under dopamine-null conditions); (b) PPL1 cells with non-punishment roles (appetitive memory extinction/reconsolidation, forgetting, novelty/familiarity in α'3, sleep/wake); (c) cotransmitter effects (NO) that invert valence in specific cells; (d) PAM DANs needed for aversive-memory extinction (omission of punishment = positive).

### Cited Findings
**Aversive PAM DANs**
- MB-M3 neurons are in the PAM cluster, induce aversive memory when activated, and terminate in the "medial tip of the β lobe" (βsp2) and β'a2; with MB-MP1 (PPL1-γ1pedc) and MB-MV1/V1 (PPL1) they form "three distinct dopamine pathways to the MB that induce aversive memory"; blocking M3 preferentially affects labile 2-h memory. — Aso et al. 2012, PLoS Genet 8:e1002768, [PMC3395599](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3395599), PMID 22807684 [FT]
- Owald 2015 repeats it: "the MB-M3 dopaminergic neurons that are required to reinforce aversive memory also innervate the tip of the β and β' lobe" — i.e. aversive and reward DANs share the β/β' tips. — [Owald 2015, PMC4416108](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4416108) [FT]
- PAM-γ3: "While the majority of PAM neurons convey reward, previous studies implied that PAM-γ3 mediates aversive reinforcement"; thermoactivation of PAM-γ3 (MB441B, MB195B) paired with odor induces aversive memory, yet blocking PAM-γ3 leaves electric-shock memory intact and *impairs* sugar-rewarded memory; PAM-γ3 mediates sugar reward by *suppressing its own activity* (ingestion lowers basal activity; inactivating basal activity substitutes for reward; AstA inhibits it). — Yamagata et al. 2016, PLoS Biol 14:e1002586, [PMC5172549](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5172549), PMID 27997541 [FT]
- Li 2020 hemibrain: "Studies of aversive olfactory learning have established that key teaching signals are provided by the PPL101 (γ1pedc), PPL103 (γ2α'1), and PAM12 (γ3) DANs"; PAM12 (γ3) has two subtypes, PAM12-md (inputs shared with positive-valence PAM DANs) and PAM12-dd (inputs shared with negative-valence PPL1 DANs). — [Li 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7909955) [FT]
- Valence inversion without dopamine: in a dopamine-null (TH mutant) background, activating PPL1-γ1pedc and PAM-γ5/PAM-β'2a produced memory of *inverted* valence, attributed to nitric oxide as an opposing cotransmitter (proposed to induce potentiation); PPL1-γ2α'1 stayed aversive in the TH mutant (cotransmitter of the same sign as dopamine); PPL1-α3 and PAM-β'1 gave no significant memory in the TH mutant. — Aso et al. 2019, eLife 8:e49257, [PMC6948953](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6948953) [FT]
- Aversive-memory extinction requires PAM (reward-type) DANs: "Extinction of appetitive memory in flies requires ... PPL1 ... many of which can provide punishment teaching signals" whereas "aversive memory extinction required PAM cluster DANs, many of which can provide reward teaching signals"; blocking PAM (R58E02) abolished CS+-driven extinction, and "omission of punishment is remembered as a positive experience." — Felsenberg et al. 2018, Cell 175:709-722 (PMID 30245010), [PMC6198041](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6198041) [FT]
- Lewis 2015 (Curr Biol 25:2203; PMID 26299514) [ABS]: glutamatergic MBON and PAM DANs with overlapping innervation in β'2 integrate food odor and suppress *innate* CO2 avoidance — PAM-β'2 acting in innate (non-learned) modulation.

**Non-aversive roles for PPL1 / mixed roles**
- Appetitive memory extinction and reconsolidation use PPL1: blocking PPL1 DANs during CS+ re-exposure impairs extinction; reconsolidation of reward memory requires MB-MV1/PPL1-γ2α'1 during CS- exposure; V2α/V2α' (cholinergic) MBONs drive these aversively reinforcing DANs. — Felsenberg 2017, [PMC5392358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5392358) [FT]
- Hunger/satiety gating: "negatively reinforcing dopaminergic neurons also provide the inhibitory control of satiety over appetitive memory expression" (via MBON-γ1pedc>α/β = MVP2 GABAergic feed-forward inhibition). — Perisse et al. 2016, Neuron 90:1086-1099, PMID 27210550 [ABS]; Aso 2014b notes DAN input to γ1/pedc "suppresses expression of appetitive memory in fed flies" [FT-partial].
- α'3 (PPL1-type vertical-lobe compartment): novel odors drive strong α'3 MBON activity, rapidly suppressed on repeated exposure, requiring odor-evoked activity of the DAN innervating this compartment; α'3 MBON activation produces an alerting response — a novelty/familiarity function rather than simple punishment. — Hattori et al. 2017, Cell 169:956-969, PMID 28502772 [ABS]
- Sleep/wake: Sitaraman et al. 2015 identified wake-promoting DANs that activate wake-promoting MBONs (glutamatergic); in the retrieved full text the stimulation experiments are on PAM neurons (ATP/P2X2 on PAM with MBON GCaMP). — Front Neural Circuits 9:73, [PMC4637407](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4637407), PMID 26617493 [FT; cluster identity of all wake-promoting DANs not confirmed in my search]. Berry et al. 2015 Cell, PMC4671826: "Sleep facilitates memory by blocking dopamine neuron-mediated forgetting" [title only, not read].
- Appetitive LTM: PAM-α1 ↔ MBON-α1 recurrent loop (Ichinose 2015 [ABS]); PPL1 DANs (MB-MP1 and MB-V1-type pairs) gate LTM via slow oscillations — Plaçais et al. 2012 Nat Neurosci 15:592, cited in the reference list of Aso & Rubin 2016 and Aso 2019 [only the reference entry seen; claim content not read].
- Cervantes-Sandoval 2017: Kenyon cells and PPL1 DANs form reciprocal axo-axonic synapses (positive-feedback loop; KC stimulation excites PPL1 DANs) — PPL1 compartments listed γ1-2, α'3, α3, α2α'2, γ1ped. [FT] eLife 6:e23789.
- Takemura 2017 (α-lobe EM connectome, eLife 6:e26975, PMID 28718765): only 6% of KC>MBON synapses receive a direct DAN synapse; new KC>DAN and DAN>MBON synapses; DAN activation causes slow MBON depolarisation and "can weaken memory recall." [ABS]

### Inferences
- The "PAM=reward" rule fails at compartment level in β/β'2 (MB-M3 aversive vs OAMB-sugar reward DANs share tips), γ3 (activation aversive, suppression = reward; per Li 2020 counted among aversive teaching signals) and potentially γ5/β'2a (valence-inverted without dopamine). The "PPL1=punishment" rule fails for roles in appetitive consolidation/extinction/reconsolidation, forgetting, novelty and sleep, but there the PPL1 cell is still *writing aversive-direction plasticity*; the non-aversive behavioral outcome comes from circuit context (e.g. extinction), not from a flipped sign of the dopamine rule. This matters for a depression-only simulator: the model can reproduce PPL1-driven extinction as depression of approach-MBON drive only if the feedback loops (MBON→DAN) are wired.

- I did not retrieve Yamagata 2015 PNAS or Huetteroth 2015 full text (PMC captcha / not OA XML); their compartment claims rest on abstracts.
- Which of the PAM compartments β1, β2, β'1 have clean *reward-only* evidence was not established from texts I read.

## 3. MBON neurotransmitters, approach/avoidance, and whether transmitter tracks DAN valence

### Takeaway
Aso 2014b: valence of MBON *activation* is "highly correlated with MBON transmitter type" — all aversion-eliciting MBONs were glutamatergic; all attraction-eliciting MBONs were GABAergic or cholinergic. Anatomically, glutamatergic MBONs sit in the horizontal/medial lobes (PAM compartments), cholinergic MBONs in the vertical lobes (PPL1 compartments), GABAergic MBONs at the intersection. The rule is explicitly *not exceptionless* and the Aso counts are by cell type, not by cell; I found no published tally that matches the project's MaleCNS counts (15/20, 19/28).

### Cited Findings
- 34 MBONs of 21 types per hemisphere; "cholinergic MBONs in the vertical (α and α') lobes, glutamatergic MBONs in the medial (β, β', and γ) lobes, and GABAergic MBONs in an area of the lobes at the intersection." Transmitter counts by immunostaining: 7 glutamatergic, 4 GABAergic, 8 cholinergic types; 2 types (in the VT-GAL4 collection) undetermined. — Aso 2014a Fig. 9, [Europe PMC full text PMC4273437](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4273437) [FT]. Totals: 7+4+8+2 = 21.
- Cell-count context: "Fourteen MBON cell types consist of only one cell per hemisphere, six types contain two cells, and one type eight cells per hemisphere." (34 = 14 + 12 + 8.) — Aso 2014a [FT]. Consequence: counting cells vs types will give different "15 of 20"-style ratios; the 8-cell type (MBON-β'1 per tool; identification not verified in text I searched) is a candidate for dominating cell-level counts.
- Behaviour: "all the MBONs whose activation resulted in avoidance were glutamatergic, whereas all the attractive MBONs were cholinergic or GABAergic"; "Activation of glutamatergic MBONs repelled flies and promoted wakefulness, whereas activation of GABAergic or cholinergic MBONs attracted flies and promoted sleep." — Aso 2014b [FT]
- Named examples (Aso 2014b): glutamatergic = MBON-γ5β'2a, β'2mp, β'2mp_bilateral, γ4>γ1γ2, β1>α, α1 (MB310C "labels the glutamatergic MBON-α1"); GABAergic = MBON-γ1pedc>α/β, γ3, γ3β'1 (and β'1 listed as a GABAergic compartment in Aso 2014a Fig. legend); cholinergic = MBON-γ2α'1, α'2, α3 (the "V3/V4" cluster), plus V2 cluster (α2sc/α'3-type). — Aso 2014b, Aso 2014a legends [FT]
- Explicit exceptions inside Aso 2014b: (i) MBON-γ1pedc>α/β is GABAergic and attractive when activated but required for *aversive* odor and visual memory; (ii) the two MBON-α3 driver lines gave inconsistent results; (iii) MBON-γ2α'1 had mixed effects; (iv) inactivating MBON-α'2 caused aversive ethanol memory to persist at 24 h. — [Aso 2014b](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04580) [FT-partial]
- Opposition rule: MBON activation valence "was, in general, opposite to that of the memory induced by dopaminergic input to the corresponding MB compartments" (e.g. MBON-γ1pedc>α/β and MBON-γ2α'1 attract while PPL1 input there is punishing; glutamatergic MBONs repel while PAM input to their compartments induces appetitive memory). — Aso 2014b Discussion [FT-partial]
- Li 2020: "Dendrites of glutamatergic MBONs arborize in the medial compartments of the horizontal lobes, which are also innervated by reward-representing PAM DANs. Most cholinergic MBONs arborize in the vertical lobes ... innervated by punishment-representing PPL1-DANs. GABAergic MBONs also arborize in compartments innervated by punishment-representing DANs." Hemibrain has 20 "typical" MBON types (dendrites in the lobes) + 14 "atypical"; GABAergic MBON10 and MBON20 were reclassified as atypical; antibody vs predicted-transmitter agreement 17 of 18; glutamate assumed inhibitory gives a "push-pull" with cholinergic MBONs on shared targets. — [Li 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7909955) [FT]
- CAUTION on a likely source typo: the same Li 2020 paper has one sentence reading "Activation of cholinergic and glutamatergic MBONs has been associated with opposing behaviors, avoidance and approach, respectively" — this is reversed relative to Aso 2014b (glutamatergic = avoidance). Do not cite that sentence. [FT]
- Note the Aso 2014b claim that GABAergic MBONs sit in "compartments innervated by punishment-representing DANs" (Li 2020) conflicts with Aso 2014's placement of MBON-γ3/γ3β'1/β'1 in PAM compartments (γ3, β'1 are PAM-innervated). So GABAergic MBONs are split between clusters: γ1pedc (PPL1) vs γ3, γ3β'1, β'1 (PAM). — Aso 2014b statement of sleep-promoting GABAergic MBON-γ3/γ3β'1 [FT] vs Li 2020 summary [FT]; flagged as a **disagreement**.
- Plasticity directionality at MBON level: aversive learning depresses CS+ drive to approach-directing MBONs (cholinergic MBON-V2α, GABAergic MVP2/MBON-γ1pedc>α/β) and *potentiates* CS+ responses in avoidance-promoting M4β'/M6 (MBON-β'2mp, MBON-γ5β'2a); reward learning reverses this (Owald 2015, Perisse 2016, Felsenberg 2018). — [Felsenberg 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6198041) [FT]; Owald 2015 [FT]: "the activation of these output neurons by the conditioned odor is depressed by reward learning and potentiated by aversive learning."

### Inferences
- The transmitter–valence relation at the *activation* level tracks the transmitter (Glu → avoid; ACh/GABA → approach) in the original screen, and the compartment-DAN valence tracks transmitter via anatomy (Glu in PAM compartments; ACh in PPL1 compartments). The project's rule "MBON readout sign set by DAN cluster" is therefore an approximation: it matches cholinergic/glutamatergic MBONs, but misassigns GABAergic MBONs in PAM compartments (γ3, γ3β'1, β'1) if those are approach-driving (they are GABAergic and Aso's rule says GABAergic/cholinergic = attraction when activated; behavioural activation phenotypes for each individual GABAergic PAM-compartment MBON were not directly read).
- The project's reported MaleCNS counts (PAM compartments: 15/20 MBONs inhibitory; PPL1: 19/28 cholinergic) are *consistent in direction* with the literature expectation (majority Glu+GABA in PAM compartments; majority ACh in PPL1 compartments) but are not a "perfect" separation: ~25% of PAM-compartment MBONs and ~32% of PPL1-compartment MBONs are on the "wrong" transmitter. The literature itself documents such cross-cluster cases (GABA MBONs in PAM compartments; GABA MBON-γ1pedc>α/β in PPL1; Glu terminals of MBON-γ4>γ1γ2 and MBON-β1>α inside γ1/γ2/α1-α3). I could not verify the MaleCNS numbers themselves (not in any retrieved source).
- Caveat on "inhibitory transmitters": GABA and glutamate are both treated as inhibitory on downstream targets in the fly MB literature (Li 2020 "assuming an inhibitory role for glutamate"; Owald 2015 "potentially inhibitory"), but glutamate has both inhibitory and excitatory receptors on most cells (Li 2020), so "Glu = inhibitory" is an assumption, not a fact.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a per-MBON-type table of transmitter, compartment and activation phenotype as text (Aso 2014a Table 1 / 2014b figures are images/tables not captured). A cell-level (not type-level) tabulation from Aso data is therefore not provided.
- No source I read tests the 15/20 and 19/28 style counts; the numbers need to be checked in the project's own MaleCNS data.

## 4. Plasticity: depression vs potentiation; DopR1 vs DopR2; backward pairing / relief learning

### Takeaway
Synaptic *depression* of the KC→MBON synapse of the CS+ odor is the best-demonstrated direct physiological mechanism (Hige 2015 Neuron; Aso & Rubin 2016 summary). But bidirectional plasticity is now documented: dopamine receptor DopR1 vs DopR2 drive depression vs potentiation depending on odor-dopamine order (Handler 2019), NO as a cotransmitter implicated in potentiation (Aso 2019), and "potentiation" of avoidance-MBON responses after aversive learning is reported (Owald 2015; Felsenberg 2018) though possibly via disinhibition. Order dependence is real: dopamine *before* the odor (20-60 s) writes the opposite-valence memory.

### Cited Findings
- Hige et al. 2015 Neuron: "Pairing an odor with activation of specific dopamine neurons induces both learning and odor-specific synaptic depression. The plasticity induction strictly depends on the temporal order of the two stimuli"; dopamine action "confined to and distinct across different anatomical compartments"; sparse-code overlap sets generalisation; reported ~80% average reduction in synaptic strength (press quote from Turner via CSHL/Technology Networks). Recordings from MBON-γ1pedc>α/β with the forward-pairing protocol (4 light pulses at 2 Hz starting 0.2 s after CS+ onset). — Hige et al., Neuron 88:985-998, PMID 26637800; [Europe PMC abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26637800); [CSHL repository](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/32022/) [ABS; PMC full text blocked, so the specific backward-pairing result for MBON-γ1pedc was NOT verified]
- Aso & Rubin 2016 uses Hige 2015 as the mechanistic base: "Pairing activation of PPL1-γ1pedc with odor has been shown to depress the subsequent spiking rate of the MBON from the γ1pedc compartments in response to the trained odor"; they propose this single DAN "can bi-directionally alter the strength of KC-MBON synapses" depending on odor-driven KC activity and timing; they do not use the terms depression/potentiation otherwise and do not discuss DopR1/DopR2 by name (only "KCs express four distinct dopamine receptors", citing Crocker 2016, possibly "deployed differently in each compartment"). Editor/reviewer exchange in the same document cites DopR1 rescue in γ KCs sufficient for aversive memory (Qin 2012). — [Aso & Rubin 2016](https://elifesciences.org/articles/16135) [FT]
- Timing rules (Aso & Rubin 2016): PPL1-γ1pedc stimulation within a 30-s window after onset of a 10-s odor presentation formed aversive memory; "DAN stimulation that precedes odor presentation by 20 to 60 s induced an appetitive memory (positive PI)"; for appetitive (PAM) memory the best dopamine window was "shifted slightly later" relative to odor, interpreted as an adaptation to the time for converting ingested food into reward. They propose the timing dependence could reflect downstream biochemical cascade dynamics. [FT]
- Relief learning confirmed: "Activation of PPL1-γ1pedc 30 s prior to odor onset induced appetitive memory (known as 'relief learning')" and NO is not needed for it; "timing dependent inversion of long-term-depression of KC-to-MBON synapses to long-term-potentiation is mediated by different dopamine receptors" (citing Handler 2019). — Aso 2019 [FT]
- Handler et al. 2019: odor-dopamine associations "can be written and reversed on a trial-by-trial basis depending on the temporal relationship"; reversals in learned attraction "correlate with bidirectional neural plasticity in the mushroom body"; "DopR1 and DopR2 contribute to this temporal sensitivity by coupling to distinct second messengers and directing either synaptic depression or potentiation." — Cell 178:60-75, PMID 31230716, [Europe PMC abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31230716) [ABS only; compartment/MBON identity, the exact delay window and which receptor does which were not verified]
- Cohn, Morantte & Ruta 2015, Cell 163:1742-1755 (PMID 26687359): dopaminergic inputs "modulate synaptic transmission with exquisite spatial specificity, allowing individual neurons to differentially convey olfactory signals to each of their postsynaptic targets"; the DANs "function as an interconnected network ... to coordinate synaptic plasticity." A later commentary (Waddell 2016, Curr Biol, per FlyBase record) is reported by a search summary as listing Hige 2015 and Cohn 2015 as evidence that dopamine drives both depression and facilitation at MB output synapses. [ABS; the specific facilitation/depression results were NOT read — PMC full text blocked]
- Owald 2015 Neuron: M4/6 glutamatergic MBON responses to conditioned odor are depressed by reward learning and potentiated by aversive learning; "we do not know that the increased odor drive after training reflects synaptic potentiation between odor-specific KCs and the M4β'/MBON-β'2mp neurons"; aversive learning "decreased unconditioned odor-evoked neurotransmission from the γ neurons ... would mirror a relative increase in the response to the reinforced odor." — [PMC4416108](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4416108) [FT]
- Felsenberg 2018: DopR1-dependent cAMP plasticity "ultimately presynaptic depression of specific output synapses"; "Aversive learning depresses the relative conditioned odor drive from KCs to MBONs whose activity favors approach behavior, whereas reward learning weakens connections onto MBONs directing avoidance"; potentiation of CS+ responses in M4β'/M6 axons "could result from release of CS+ specific feedforward inhibition through MVP2 neurons"; extinction-driven plasticity in M6 "neutralizes potentiation of the trained-odor response." — [PMC6198041](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6198041) [FT]
- Felsenberg 2017: "Reward memories are written by DANs that innervate the horizontal MB lobes, which depress odor drive to MBON pathways that direct avoidance"; memory reduced after reconsolidation-related depression. [FT]
- Aso 2014b on mechanism: "dopamine modulation led to synaptic depression of the outputs of the KCs representing the CS+ stimulus" is "most easily explained" (not demonstrated); alternatives include dopamine potentiating release of an inhibitory cotransmitter (sNPF), and "multiple mechanisms are likely to be used." Vertebrate basal-ganglia analogy: both potentiation and depression. — [Aso 2014b](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04580) [FT-partial / FT]
- Aso 2019: NO as an opposing cotransmitter; "valence-inversion phenotype ... can be most easily explained if NO induces synaptic potentiation between odor-activated KCs and their target MBONs"; applies to PPL1-γ1pedc and PAM-γ5/PAM-β'2a. [FT]
- Cervantes-Sandoval 2017: "Activation of γ1pedc DAn induces both learning and odor-specific synaptic depression in MBOnγ1pedc when paired with only a 1 s odor presentation, whereas similar plasticity in the α2α'2 compartment requires 1 min odor presentation" — compartment-specific plasticity kinetics. [FT]
- Perisse 2016: aversive learning causes "a persistent depression of the conditioned odor drive" to the GABAergic MVP2 (MBON-γ1pedc>α/β). [ABS]
- Takemura 2017: DAN activation produces "a slow depolarization of the MBON" at DAN>MBON synapses and "can weaken memory recall" — a non-KC route for dopamine to alter MBON output. [ABS]

### Inferences
- A depression-only rule applied to KC→MBON synapses with polarity set by cluster reproduces the dominant, best-documented direction (CS+ drive to approach-MBONs depressed by PPL1/aversive; to avoidance-MBONs depressed by PAM/reward). It cannot reproduce: (i) relief/backward-pairing learning (needs a time-window rule that flips sign or recruits the opposite MBON class), (ii) DopR2/NO-type potentiation, (iii) the "potentiated" CS+ responses in avoidance-MBONs after aversive learning if these are genuine synaptic potentiation rather than disinhibition via MVP2. Whether to include a feed-forward-inhibition MBON→MBON route is the crucial design question: the evidence for disinhibition (Felsenberg 2018, Perisse 2016) supports a depression-only plus inhibitory-interneuron model.
- Because Aso & Rubin 2016 treats a single DAN type as able to write either valence with timing alone, the cluster-sets-valence assumption is a simplification even within the canonical PPL1-γ1pedc.

### Gaps
- Handler 2019 and Hige 2015 (Neuron) full text not retrieved: exact time windows, which receptor drives depression vs potentiation, and the backward-pairing outcome in MBON-γ1pedc>α/β were not verified.
- Cohn 2015 specifics (what sort of dopamine-induced modulation, which PAM/PPL1 types) not read.
